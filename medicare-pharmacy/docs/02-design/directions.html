MediCare Pharmacy · Phase 3 · Gate G4

Three directions for the counter screen

Each direction shows the same POS sale: five medicines, counter 3, the evening rush. Augmentin is selected because its batch expires in 47 days. Risek was picked by a 2D DataMatrix scan, and the rest by FEFO. Prices are illustrative. The directions differ in density, type, tone and how the keyboard is shown, not only in colour. All three are buildable with owner-drawn Windows Forms controls on both Windows 7 and Windows 10/11.

Type, shape, density

  • Atkinson Hyperlegible for UI. It was designed for low-vision readers, and ambiguous glyphs (I/l/1, 0/O) are distinct.
  • IBM Plex Mono for amounts, batches and invoice numbers.
  • 6 px radius, 1 px borders, no shadows except the scan field's focus halo.
  • Medium density: 40 px rows; 13-15 px text; total at 40 px.

References borrowed

  • Square POS: one dominant amount, receipt-like cart.
  • Fluent 2: desktop focus visuals and quiet surfaces.
  • Mint POS: expiry colour bands, here always paired with days left.

Strong at

  • Readability across an 8-hour shift and on cheap 1366×768 screens.
  • Looks credible to owners and inspectors.
  • Easiest to theme for the A4 reports and receipts.

Costs

  • Shows fewer lines than C before scrolling (about 9 vs 14 at 768 px).
  • Light surfaces need a separate dark theme later (tokens make it a palette swap).

Type, shape, density

  • IBM Plex Sans for UI, IBM Plex Mono for every figure: one family with two voices.
  • Square 2 px corners, full grid lines, keycap strip with real key names.
  • High density: 26 px rows; about 14 lines visible; 12-13 px text.
  • Alt-underlined menu mnemonics, like classic desktop software.

References borrowed

  • Marg ERP / Tally: F-key muscle memory, party/voucher vocabulary.
  • Spreadsheet grids: column lines and a highlighter-yellow active row.
  • Pharmacy dispensing labels: uppercase item names, pack codes.

Strong at

  • Most information per screen; batch, rack, cost and margin all visible.
  • Zero retraining for staff coming from Marg-style tools.
  • Cheapest to build in WinForms (closest to native grid behaviour).

Costs

  • Small text is harder for older staff and at 1366×768.
  • Looks dated to new hires; less forgiving for beginners.
  • Showing cost and margin on the counter screen needs a permission gate.

Recommendation

A, with C's keycap strip and a density switch

Use Clear Dispensary as the system: hyperlegible type, calm teal, light surfaces that suit both the counter and the back office. Borrow two things from Keyboard Ledger: the persistent F-key strip at the foot of the POS, and a per-user "Compact" density (32 px rows, about 12 lines) for staff who prefer more on screen. Keep B's 64 px total as a customer-facing second display later, if the shop adds pole displays. The choice is yours: pick A, B, C, or name a blend.

Batch and expiry printed on request · Thank you

The receipt is the same in every direction. Urdu goes through the Windows printer driver as rendered text, because most thermal printers cannot shape Arabic script themselves. Two font options are under test for Windows 7 shaping:

  • Naskh shapes reliably under Windows 7 text rendering and stays legible at 203 dpi.
  • Nastaliq is the traditional Urdu style, but it needs more line height and shapes less reliably on old systems. It is enabled only where the S00 spike passes.
  • Medicine names in Urdu are optional per medicine. The footer lines are set in Settings.
  • The licence number, address and phone shown here are placeholders for the owner to supply.
